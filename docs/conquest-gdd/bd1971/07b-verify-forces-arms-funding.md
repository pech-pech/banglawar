# 07b Verification: forces, arms, funding (Bangladesh 1971)

Purpose: check eight items from files 01, 02 and 03 against stronger sources. Neutral and factual; this is a real and painful event.

Method and honest limits:
- Banglapedia (en.banglapedia.org) still returned an empty page ("Hello World") for every article tried this session (Mujibnagar Government, Operation Jackpot, Razakar, Al-Badr, Swadhin Bangla Betar Kendra). So Banglapedia is NOT used as a verifying source; only its titles appear in search results. Library of Congress (countrystudies.us page tried) covered only 1966-1970. Britannica returned HTTP 403. The BMJ page returned 403, PubMed Central showed a captcha, and the Open University page returned 403.
- Pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so wording is paraphrased. Where only a search-result summary was seen (page not opened) it is flagged "search summary".
- "Independent" below means a source that is not a copy of the same Wikipedia text. Most independent sources here are The Daily Star (Dhaka), The Business Standard, FRUS (US State Department primary documents) and a Pakistani-officer memoir quoted in the Daily Star. No page contained instructions addressed to the researcher.
- Verdict scale: VERIFIED, PARTLY, CONTRADICTED, NOT FOUND.

Headline corrections to our earlier notes:
1. The radio did NOT run from Agartala until 6 December. It moved on to Calcutta (Ballygunge) on 25 May 1971. 6 December is the rename to Bangladesh Betar.
2. The "Calcutta arms market" list (Italian howitzers, Alouette helicopter, DC-3, Otter) is doubtful as a purchase list: the three aircraft were donated by Indian authorities (one DC-3 from the Maharaja of Jodhpur) per the Kilo Flight page. Do not use the howitzer/aircraft list as "bought".
3. Razakar formation: mid-1971 reconstitution of the Ansar, ordinance 2 August 1971 (most sources); one search result says 1 June. Treat the ordinance date as 2 August.
4. The sabotage totals (231 / 122 / 90) are real as a quotation: Salik, Witness to Surrender, p. 104, but they are one Pakistani officer's tally, not an audited count.

FRUS embassy reporting: over 500,000 in early May (Indian estimate), 5.4 million by 11 June. ## Item 4. Razakar, Al-Badr, Al-Shams

| Group | Verdict | One-line, sourced | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Razakars | PARTLY (date conflict) | Pro-Pakistan volunteer force; reported formed in May 1971 by Jamaat-linked leaders (A. K. M. Yusuf) from the Ansar at a camp with 96 Jamaat men (Daily Star); the Ansar was reconstituted as Razakars in June; the East Pakistan Razakars Ordinance promulgated 2 August 1971 by Lt Gen Tikka Khan created a voluntary force trained and equipped by the provincial government; on 7 September 1971 it was brought under the army via a Defence Ministry ordinance, under Maj Gen Mohammed Jamshed; about 50,000 members in brigades of 3,000-4,000 (Wikipedia). One search result gives 1 June for the ordinance; that conflicts and is unconfirmed. | [24][25] |
| Al-Badr | PARTLY | Armed wing drawn from the Islami Chhatra Sangha (Jamaat student wing), described in a Bangladesh International Crimes Tribunal judgment (quoted by the Daily Star); constituted around September 1971 under General Niazi's command per search summary; the same judgment cites Husain Haqqani for its recognition in May 1971. Date therefore sits between May and September 1971. | [26][25] |
| Al-Shams | PARTLY | Paramilitary group of general-public members of Jamaat-e-Islami, Muslim League, Nizam-e-Islami and similar parties, per the same tribunal judgment; formed alongside Al-Badr; no exact date found. A fourth group, Al-Mujahid, was drawn from Urdu-speaking Biharis per the same judgment. | [26] |
| "Dissolved 16 December" and "Razakar status official" | PARTLY | The 2 August ordinance and 7 September army integration make the Razakars an official auxiliary; Al-Badr and Al-Shams official status was not confirmed. Dissolution date not re-checked. | [24] |

## Item 6. Operation Jackpot

| Claim | Verdict | Corrected statement | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dates: naval phase 25 April to 15 August, main attacks 16 August | PARTLY | Main attacks were in the night 14 to 15 August 1971 (after midnight) according to the commander, Commodore Abdul Wahed Chowdhury, in the Daily Star; the Wikipedia page lists the large simultaneous attacks on 16 August. Treat as "15-16 August, depending on source". Training began about 21 May at a camp in Plassey, West Bengal (Daily Star; file 02 had camp C2P from 23 May, same site). | [32][33][12] |
| Ports: Chittagong, Chandpur, Narayanganj, Mongla | VERIFIED | Chowdhury: "We planned to destroy four places: Chittagong, Mongla, Chandpur and Narayanganj." | [32][33] |
| Commandos | PARTLY | Wikipedia: initial 300, 499 trained, 160 in the first batch for the August attacks; Daily Star (Khalilur Rahman): first batch 160, 148 deployed; Chowdhury: about 500 trained overall, 60 in Chittagong. Figures from participants differ; use "about 160 in the first strike, about 500 trained". | [33][34][12] |
| Ships sunk: 65 vs 126 | PARTLY: both sourced, different windows and definitions | 126 ships, coasters and ferries "sunk or damaged" August to December 1971 (Wikipedia, no named source). At least 65 vessels by November 1971 (15 Pakistani ships, 11 coasters, 7 gunboats, 11 barges, 2 tankers, 19 river craft), cited to Jacob 2003 p. 91. At least 100,000 tons "sunk or crippled" cited to Roy 1995. Participant accounts: 45 ships sunk (Chowdhury interview, Daily Star), 26 ships on the night of 16 August, "more than 100" over nine months (Daily Star, Rahman). So 65 = sunk by end November (Jacob), 126 = sunk or damaged to December (unattributed); 45 and 100+ are participant claims. Present as "dozens to over a hundred vessels, by source". | [12][32][34] |
| Casualties of the commandos | NOT CONSISTENT | Wikipedia (file 01): 8 killed and 34 wounded over the campaign; Chowdhury (Daily Star) claims "zero casualties" in his operation. Unresolved. | [12][32] |

## Item 7. Sabotage totals (231 bridges, 122 railway lines, 90 power installations)

| Claim | Verdict | Corrected statement | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 231 bridges, 122 railway lines, 90 electric installations | VERIFIED as a quotation; the numbers themselves are UNVERIFIED | The wording is "Their sabotage inventory included damage to, or destruction of, 231 bridges, 122 railway lines and 90 electric installations", page 104 of Siddiq Salik, Witness to Surrender (Salik was a Pakistan Army public-relations officer posted to East Pakistan from January 1970), as quoted in the Daily Star "My blood will hasten the liberation of my sacred land".
