## 1. Purpose and what a "sign-off" means

**Purpose.** The theme pack is built from tertiary sources (mostly Wikipedia and news features read through a summarising tool; 07a-07c improved many facts, but several remain single-source). The game concerns a war within living memory, remembered by many as a genocide. Reviewers exist so that no fact, term, spelling, slogan, symbol or tone ships unless a qualified person from the relevant community has seen it and the decision is recorded.

**A sign-off is** a written statement by one named reviewer (or a pseudonym by their request) that, for a stated list of items and a stated version of the files, they:
1. reviewed each item;
2. gave a verdict per item: ACCEPT, ACCEPT WITH CHANGE (the change is written down), REJECT (cut it), or CANNOT JUDGE (outside their knowledge);
3. gave a source or a reason for every verdict other than ACCEPT.

**A sign-off is not:**
- an endorsement of the game, the studio or any claim in marketing;
- an approval of items they were not shown;
- agreement that the whole subject is appropriate for a game (they may decline to say);
- permission to use their name, institution or photo (that needs a separate written yes; section 3.4).

**Scope of a sign-off is per item and per version.** If an item's text changes after sign-off, that item is back to PENDING (section 7.1).

## 3. How to approach and compensate reviewers

### 3.1 Principles
- **Ask, do not assume.** Reviewers are doing the project a favour on a painful subject. Approach through a trusted intermediary or institutional channel where possible.
- **Be honest about what the game is** and what it is not (section 4.3), and show the human-cost design first. Reviewers who object to the whole idea are told "thank you"; this is not a failure.
- **No pressure to endorse.** The brief states that a critical review is the most useful review. A reviewer may stop, narrow their scope or decline at any time without giving a reason.
- **Review the work, not the person.** Never ask a reviewer to speak for their community, party or institution.

### 3.2 Scope and time estimate (planning numbers, author's judgement)
| Role | Scope | Time |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Questionnaire A (about 60 facts and about 20 wording items) plus the encyclopedia entries | 12-20 hours over 3-4 weeks; offer to split into two passes |
| R2 | Questionnaire B (about 80-120 terms and strings) | 8-12 hours over 2-3 weeks |
| R3 | Questionnaire C (tone, about 15 texts, image limits) | 4-6 hours, plus an optional 60-minute call |
| R4 | A short selection (remembrance, end screens, refugee entry) | 1-2 hours |
| R5 | Questionnaire D | 5-8 hours |
| R6 | Questionnaire E (layout, placeholders, fonts) | 6-10 hours |

### 3.3 Compensation and credit options (owner chooses; offer, never require)
- **Paid fee** (recommended for R1, R2, R6; standard professional rate for the person's country and field; agreed in writing before work starts; paid regardless of verdict).
- **Honorarium or donation** to a cause the reviewer names (for R3, R4, where personal payment is awkward).
- **Credit options** (the reviewer picks; none is the default): named in credits as "Historical review: Name" (never worded as endorsement); credited as "Reviewed by an independent historian" with no name; no credit at all.
- **Institutional credit** only on written agreement from the institution. Wording: "Thanks for review input" and never "in partnership with", "endorsed by" or "approved by".
- **Never offer** game keys as the only payment, a revenue share contingent on sales, or anything contingent on a positive verdict.

### 3.4 Sharing terms (plain language, to be checked by a lawyer before use)
- Materials are shared as plain documents (PDF or DOCX) over a private channel the reviewer chooses. They are **confidential until release**; reviewers should not post screenshots or excerpts.
- The reviewer keeps ownership of their notes; the studio may use their corrections in the game and cite them in the evidence log.
- Their name, photo or institution is used **only** with separate written consent, revocable until release.
- Reviewer comments are not published. A short anonymised summary of "what changed because of review" may be published with their consent.
- A mutual NDA is offered only if the reviewer asks for one; keep it one page.

### 4.2 The context paragraph (all reviewers)
> "1971: The Liberation War" is a single-player strategy game in which the player organises the Bangladesh Forces across ten sector areas from 26 March to 16 December 1971, at three days per turn, against an AI playing the Pakistan Army's Eastern Command. It uses a generic strategy engine; the 1971 content is a "theme pack" of labels, a calendar, a map, dated historical context lines and an encyclopedia. The research behind it is secondary and partly unverified; the purpose of this review is to find what is wrong, contested, offensive or badly worded before anyone sees it. Nothing is final.

### 4.3 How the game treats the human cost (Hybrid H; say this to every reviewer)
- **No civilians in the simulation.** No civilian unit, resource, village, refugee stock, relief mechanic, score or event. A test checks this in the code.
- **Attackable places are military installations only** (bases, cantonments, depots, river landings, bridges). Towns appear as map names. A site named after a real town can be captured but never destroyed.
- **No body counts, no kill scores.** Losses appear as strength points. Nothing is rewarded for damage dealt.
- **"Personnel", not "people".** A supply shortage is shown as personnel standing down and going home. Caveat to disclose: the rules cannot tell going home from dying; the meaning is in the wording only (SC 3.1, 06 R-14; 09 section C.1).
- **Capture.** A captured position keeps its staffing count, so the opponent's personnel are counted as the player's volunteers; the text never narrates this (06 R-7). The owner dropped the "personnel never change sides" switch on 2026-10-03. This is a disclosed, text-level mitigation, not a rule (SC 3.1 condition 5).
- **Residual risk, stated plainly (Hybrid H).** Shortage and capture meanings rest on wording alone: shortage is shown as volunteers going home, and no one is killed by a shortage, but the rules cannot tell going home from dying. The wording is checked by V-04, V-05 and V-21 and reviewed by the historian (A.8).
- **Atrocity is history, never play.** It is mentioned only in remembrance and encyclopedia text, plain and sourced; sexual violence is not depicted, and the contested range is named only in the encyclopedia, reviewer-gated.
- **The human story is in text:** a remembrance line on the menu, a first-launch note, dignified end screens, credits with sources, and an encyclopedia including the language movement, the March non-cooperation movement, refugees and the contested casualty ranges. Dated context lines in the turn report can be switched off; the remembrance cannot.
- **The opponent is a professional army, not a villain.** Same art quality, same rules, surrender (no annihilation); Razakar, Al-Badr and Al-Shams are never units.
- **No real person is named in game text.** (Reviewers may say whether cameos are wanted.)

### 4.4 Decisions already made vs decisions the reviewer may influence

**Made (owner), reviewer may comment but the structure will not change in review:** Hybrid H treatment; no civilians in the simulation; the two sides' roles; one turn = three days from 26 March 1971; 89 turns; December event on 3 December; own-government patron with tax 0; no real names in text; draw at the deadline; surrender win test (the capital garrison, or 3 of the 6 fortress positions while the opponent holds at most a quarter of its 20 starting garrison positions; owner set K = 6 and N = 3; 4 of 6 is still offered, 08 Q5); loss after 15 turns with no base area.

**Reviewer may influence (examples; the cut/keep decision follows section 7):** any wording; whether an item ships, ships as a range, or is cut; the fortress list; the declaration and radio wording; spellings (period vs present-day); whether "Freedom-fighter section" is acceptable; the end-screen texts; the surrender entry and picture; whether to include cameo mentions of leaders; whether the game should exist in its current form (R1-R4 may say it should not; this is recorded, section 7.2).

## 7. Decision rules

### 7.1 When reviewers disagree
1. Record both verdicts and sources in the log. Status OPEN-DISPUTE.
2. Fact disagreements: ask each reviewer for their source. If R1 and R5 disagree on a fact, R1's view prevails for what the game states as fact **only if** R1 provides a source; otherwise show the attributed claims side by side ("X says A; Y says B") or cut the item.
3. Language disagreements: R2 decides spelling and script. If R2 and R3 disagree on a Bengali sentence, ask for a third native reader; until then use the more conservative text or cut it.
4. Tone/sensitivity disagreements: the **more cautious** option ships (the one that is more respectful or says less). R3 and R4 outweigh game-team preferences. R5 cannot veto a Bangladeshi-community decision on remembrance wording, but R5's factual corrections on Pakistani-side statements are taken seriously and sent to R1.
5. The owner decides only after the above and records the reason in the log. The owner never overrules a REJECT on a sensitive item by shipping it; the item stays HOLD or is cut.
6. A reviewer's "the whole approach is wrong" is recorded and reported to the owner, not argued.

### 7.2 When a fact cannot be confirmed
1. **Cut it** (default for single-source, politically sensitive or unverified items; for example the 14 December Martyred Intellectuals line if not confirmed, Kilo Flight, Jessore 6 December).
2. **Show a range or an attributed claim** if at least two attributable sources exist and the reviewer accepts the wording ("estimates range from ... to ...").
3. **Mark as unchecked in development builds only** (as 05's UNVERIFIED rule): an UNVERIFIED item is blocked from release builds.
4. A generic or vaguer wording is preferred to a specific wrong one ("In July the first regular brigade was formed", not a letter or a name).
5. Single figures about casualties or strengths never appear in UI text; only in the encyclopedia figures blocks.

### 9.2 Flag and emblem conventions (from 07c; legal texts not read in full)
- The 1971 flag was a green field with a red disc holding a golden map; the plain red disc flag was adopted in January 1972; the designer credit is disputed; a 1972 order governs display and respect (penalties for disrespect) and a separate order bars the state emblem (water lily) for trade use.
- Rule for the game: no flag unless reviewed; any flag drawn is labelled 1971 or current, shown flying properly, never torn, burned, inverted, on the ground or beneath another flag; the state emblem is never used; no water-lily emblem picture next to the word "Shapla"; generic banners and invented colours elsewhere.
- Ask R3/R4/legal reader. If no clear yes: no flag.

### 9.3 Legal conventions to check (NOT RESEARCHED beyond 07c)
Flag and emblem display law; any restrictions on distortion of Liberation War history; use of the national anthem or radio songs (rights and sensitivity); trademark of slogans; copyright of the flag artwork; use of institution names and logos (Liberation War Museum, Banglapedia); store age rating and local content rules. A lawyer qualified for the relevant market gives an opinion on the specific items listed, not on the project as a whole.

### 9.4 Fallback plan: ship with those items cut
If a required sign-off is missing at the release gate:
