## What the scores say
1. **Viewpoint:** a steep, high, nearly overhead view of a bounded slab or tile field scored best (W4 and W5 both 5 on Seedream; W5 5 on Kling). The low 30-degree camera (W3) and the picture with a horizon and sky (Seedream W2) scored 2: the wider "scene" framing lost points.
2. **Colour and lighting:** the only 5/5 pair is Seedream W2: overcast, even soft light with slate blues and blue-greens. Warm sun-lit versions (dawn W1, golden W3) scored only 3. Kling's olive ground and vignette scored the lowest on both (2.6 and 2.4).
3. **Model:** Seedream 4.5 wins on overall, colour and lighting; Kling 3 Omni wins on viewpoint (cleaner framing of the world slab) at two-thirds of the price.
4. **Best overall single image:** Seedream W4 (sea-to-hills, steep overhead), with lighting the one weak score (3).

## R5b: higher camera test (sent 2026-10-04, Seedream 4.5, 30 credits, balance 4,104 to 4,074; audit passes)

Owner's request: a slightly higher viewpoint for the R5 background, with the camera angle classified for the prompt. Camera classes (elevation above the horizon; tile top ratio = 1 / sin(elevation)): A low three-quarter 20 to 30 degrees; B classic isometric 30 to 36; C high isometric 45; D steep 55 to 60; E very steep 65 to 75; F plan 90. R5 itself measured about class B. Two prompts differed only in the camera phrase.

Finding: both pictures show tile tops about twice as wide as high (about 30 degrees), so the spelled-out degrees were not honoured; the 45 degree picture was accepted because its framing still read well, while the 55 degree picture was cropped at the left and bottom. Lesson for the prompt engineer: the model ignores written-out angles; give digits and the tile-top width to height ratio.
